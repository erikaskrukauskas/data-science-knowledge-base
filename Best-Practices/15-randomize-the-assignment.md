# Randomize the assignment

Assign conditions by a random schedule so that one run does not depend on the previous run and does not predict the next one.

The NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook defines randomization as that schedule, and states: "Randomization is necessary for conclusions drawn from the experiment to be correct, unambiguous and defensible." ([A Glossary of DOE Terminology](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section7/pri7.htm), checked 2026-10-17.)

## What counts

A random assignment uses a chance mechanism you can describe. These do not count:

- Giving the first half of the units one condition because they were convenient.
- Alternating conditions in a fixed pattern that tracks time, temperature, or operator.
- Letting the unit's outcome, or a trait tied to the outcome, choose the condition.

Within a block, randomize inside the block. See [Block a known nuisance](14-block-a-known-nuisance.md).

## What to record

Record the schedule and the moment it was fixed. A schedule written down after you have seen the responses is not the assignment that protects the comparison.

An observational study has no assignment to randomize. Say that the comparison is observational, and name the confounding you cannot break. See [Name the confounding](12-name-the-confounding.md).

## What stays in the project

The random-number method and the unit list stay in the project. The reusable rule is that an arbitrary or systematic order is not a random assignment.
