# Use a tolerance interval when the question is a proportion of units

A tolerance interval aims to cover a stated proportion of future units, and a confidence interval for the mean does not.

The NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook states: "Difference between confidence and tolerance intervals Confidence limits are limits within which we expect a given population parameter, such as the mean, to lie." ([Tolerance intervals for a normal distribution](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc263.htm), checked 2026-10-19.)

## What to do

- Say the proportion of the population the interval is supposed to cover, and the confidence in that claim.
- State the distributional assumption. A normal tolerance interval is wrong for a distribution that is not normal.
- Do not report a confidence interval for the mean as the range where individual units will fall.
